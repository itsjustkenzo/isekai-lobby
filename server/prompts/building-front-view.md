# Mandatory building viewpoint reconstruction

Reconstruct the concept as a FRONT-FACING RPG building, not a flattened photograph.

- Identify the main front facade from its entrance, porch, signage, window arrangement and roof orientation. Mentally rotate the building so that this facade faces the viewer squarely.
- The vertical front wall is a straight-on elevation: horizontal foundation and eaves, vertical walls and door frames, no converging perspective lines. Only the roof is seen slightly from above, matching the game reference.
- If the concept is angled or shows two walls, use the side wall only to understand construction, depth, materials and roof geometry. Do not draw that wall as a visible side face in the output.
- Do not compress, shear, stretch, unfold or paste the front and side walls together. Do not transfer side-wall windows, doors or extensions onto the front facade merely to retain every feature in the photograph.
- Reconstruct the true frontal proportions after removing photographic foreshortening. Frontage is the width of the front facade, not the combined width of both visible walls. Preserve actual asymmetry and distinctive front-facing features; do not impose symmetry or a generic cottage silhouette.
- Preserve recognizable architecture, materials, roof form, colors, floor count and front-window relationships. Roof ridges and slopes must be reprojected into the required viewpoint rather than copied at the photo's angle.
- If part of the front is hidden, infer only restrained, structurally plausible continuation from visible evidence. Do not invent extra wings, floors, doors or decorative elements.
- Fit the reconstructed building to the assigned game canvas and footprint. Keep the required front entrance at its assigned bottom position, at a plausible character scale; adjust its placement without collapsing the facade around it.
- Never output a three-quarter, isometric or side view. Never solve a viewpoint mismatch by squashing both walls into one wide front wall.

Before finishing, check: one main frontal facade; no exposed side wall; recognizable concept architecture; roof slightly visible from above; level ground line; upright entrance; transparent background.
